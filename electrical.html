A camera job is mostly an electrical job.

Cable has to get from the corner of a terrace to a recorder two floors down, on a supply that doesn't drop out, through conduit that survives the monsoon. All of that is done here too — not handed to somebody else.

The work

What's included around the cameras.

  • Concealed conduit and casing-capping runs Chased into the wall on new construction, surface casing on finished buildings — not cable stapled along a skirting.
  • Dedicated power point and MCB for the recorder So the DVR isn't sharing a socket with a fridge or a welding point.
  • Earthing and surge protection for outdoor cameras The single most common reason outdoor cameras die in their first season.
  • UPS or inverter backup on the recorder Recording survives a power cut — which is exactly when it tends to matter.
  • Poles, brackets, junction boxes and weatherproof glanding Fabricated and fitted for gates, boundary walls and open compounds.
  • Cable routing across floors, terraces and boundary walls Including core drilling, terrace crossings and runs out to a separate guard room.
  • General electrical repairs on the same visit Loose points, dead sockets and old wiring found while the job is already open.

Why it matters who does the wiring

When the cameras and the electrical are split between two people, every later fault turns into an argument about whose side it is. Doing both means one number to call, one person accountable, and no waiting a week for an electrician to come back and finish a job that's already half open.

It also means the survey accounts for supply and cable routing from the start, so the quote doesn't grow halfway through the installation.
